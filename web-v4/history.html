The land before the town

Before 1854

A story that starts before Louisburg

The area that became Louisburg sits within a much older Indigenous history. Confederated Peoria peoples — including Peoria, Kaskaskia, Wea and Piankashaw communities — held reservation lands in this part of eastern Kansas before federal land cessions opened much of the area to non-Native settlement.

1854

Treaty, land cession and a new settlement era

On May 30, 1854, the United States concluded a treaty with the united Kaskaskia, Peoria, Piankeshaw and Wea tribes. The treaty recognized their confederation and provided for allotments and disposition of surplus reservation lands, accelerating the transition of this part of eastern Kansas into a new settlement era.

1857

John W. Chaudoins and the future townsite

The 1883 county history identifies John W. Chaudoins as the earliest pioneer of the locality. It says he purchased land near what became Louisburg in 1857, when there was not yet a wagon road laid out in the area, and built the first house on the townsite.

Louisburg's school district is organized

Louisburg's school district was formally organized in 1865, before the town itself was platted. That educational thread later led to “Old Brick,” the early high-school program and the 1926 Louisburg Rural High School.

1868–1883

A town takes shape

Founding milestone

November 10, 1868 — Louisburg is laid out.

Charles Sims, Dr. R. F. Steger and D. L. Peery surveyed and laid out the community. The City still recognizes November 10 as Louisburg's founding day. Early sources called the settlement New St. Louis or Little St. Louis before the Louisburg name took hold.

1868

A Broadway building survives from the beginning

Historic Preservation Commission research identifies 120 S. Broadway as standing since 1868, making it one of Broadway's oldest continuously used commercial buildings. Modern commercial property data independently lists an 1868 year built.

c. 1870–72

Little St. Louis becomes Louisburg

The sources agree on the renaming story but not the exact year. Cutler's 1883 history says the town was given the name Louisburg in 1870 to avoid confusion with St. Louis, Missouri; Louisburg United Methodist Church history places the change in 1871; City historical material says the name was in use by 1872. The timeline keeps the range rather than inventing certainty.

1881

The Clark House rises — later remembered as the Park House

W. H. Tawney built Louisburg's fine brick hotel in 1881 for $5,000. Cutler's 1883 history called it the Clark House, described it as well furnished and said it could accommodate 25 guests. Later Miami County museum material calls the matching 1881 Tawney hotel the Park House and places it at the northwest corner of Amity and Broadway. The matching builder, date, cost and capacity make Clark House → Park House a strong identification, but the deed-level identity is still being researched.

1882

Louisburg incorporates — and Tawney joins the first City Council

Louisburg became a third-class city on November 3, 1882. At the first city election on November 17, Samuel W. Moore was elected mayor and hotel builder W. H. Tawney was elected as one of the city's first five councilmen.

The first organized fire-protection effort

On March 15, 1895, Jesse Williams was asked to build a hook-and-ladder fire apparatus for the city — the opening chapter of the Louisburg Fire Department's recorded history.

A natural-gas well supplies a Louisburg hotel

A federal geological report recorded a 325-foot gas well at Louisburg supplying a hotel. The report does not name the hotel, so Louisburg Local does not assume it was the Clark/Park House.

1909

Fire destroys City Hall and a Broadway block

On November 18, 1909, fire destroyed City Hall, burned city records and wiped out the west-side Broadway block between Amity and South First. Reported damage was about $3,000.

A fire truck — then an organized crew

The City purchased a fire truck in 1917. In December 1919, Mayor Tronjo appointed R. Henry Reed fire chief and named an organized engine crew.

1917–43

The former hotel enters the Livingston apartment era

Miami County museum material reports that P. Jacob and Della Livingston owned the former hotel property from 1917 to 1943 and used the building as apartments. The actual deed book/page, seller and 1943 buyer have not yet been recovered, so this is preserved as museum-documented history rather than a deed-proven chain of title.

1957

A Labor Day photograph shows the old hotel still standing

A locally shared 1957 Labor Day parade photograph is captioned as the “Lee & Ida Erb Hotel.” The mansard-roof building visually matches earlier Park/Clark House imagery, but the identity has not been closed with deeds, and other genealogical material names Leo “Pop” Erb rather than Lee. The photograph is therefore preserved as evidence with the name conflict visible.

The Katy railroad era ends in Louisburg

City historical planning material says the Missouri-Kansas-Texas Railroad — the MKT or “Katy” — ended service in Louisburg during the late 1950s. Highway access increasingly carried the town's transportation story forward.

Centennial Louisburg becomes modern Louisburg

1967–68

Louisburg creates Kansas' first district library

Library District #1 was established in July 1967 and identifies itself as the first district library in Kansas. In 1968 the library moved from a two-room house on Second Street into the former Rosner Ford showroom. The building later burned, and the library moved into the adjacent building to the north.

Centennial Louisburg leaves a time capsule

Louisburg's population surges

The city's population grew from 2,576 in the 2000 Census to 4,315 in 2010 — about a 68% increase in one decade. It is one of the clearest turning points in modern Louisburg.
